## Goal

Build a cardboard and ice-cream-stick worker who pushes a wheelbarrow. As the wheels turn, an off-centre crank on the axle moves his legs so he looks like he is walking. Learn how a crank turns round-and-round motion into back-and-forth motion.

## Parts list

- 3 mm corrugated cardboard (sheets 1 and 3)
- About 12 ice-cream sticks (114 x 10 mm)
- 2 bottle caps or 65 mm wheels
- Wooden skewer or 3 mm rod (axle and pins)
- Paper straw (axle bearing)
- TT gear motor, 2xAA battery holder, slide switch (optional: skip for push mode)
- Ping-pong ball (head) and a bottle cap (hat)
- Hot glue (used by a grown-up), kid scissors, pencil

## Steps

1. Cut and fold the barrow tray (sheet 1). Fold the base tabs up and glue the side walls, back wall and sloped front flap on.
2. Glue the two axle hangers under the tray. Push the skewer axle through a paper straw, then through the hangers, and glue a bottle-cap wheel on each end.
3. Cut the sticks with sheet 2. Pin thigh + shin and upper arm + forearm with Ø3 pins so the joints swing.
4. Build the torso box (sheet 3). Glue the hands to the handle sticks and the hip bar to the barrow frame.
5. Glue a crank disc on the axle. Join its off-centre Ø3 hole to the knee with the 25 mm crank link.
6. Optional motor mode: fit the TT motor in the bracket, the 2xAA holder on its pad, and a slide switch in the wire. Or skip this and push him along.
7. Add the ping-pong ball head and the bottle-cap hat on the Ø40 brim. Test, then tune the link length or pin spots until the legs step smoothly.

## Safety

- A grown-up uses the hot glue gun and does all craft-knife cutting and hole poking.
- Snip the sharp tips off skewers.
- Batteries: put them in the right way round, never connect the wires directly together, and take them out when you have finished.
- Keep small parts away from little children.

## How it works

The crank pin sits 6 mm away from the centre of the axle. When the wheels turn, the pin travels in a circle. The 25 mm link joins the pin to the knee, so each turn pushes the knee forward and pulls it back again. The thigh swings from the hip and the legs step. Engineers use the same idea (a crank and a connecting rod) in steam trains and car engines.
